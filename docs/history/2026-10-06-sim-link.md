# SimLink: the supervisor over a simulated plant (CTRL-053, operator decision 2026-10-05: option 1)

`RobotRuntime` reaches its plant through a link. `plants/sim_link.SimLink` is the second implementation of that surface (the
first is `RtBackend` for an `arm_rt_server`): an in-process physics plant behind the same supervisor, SDK and admission. The
supervisor is the controller manager, the plant (hardware or simulation) a hardware plugin. `python -m
arm_control.supervisor.service --config ... --plant sim` selects it (default `rt`); no launch file or unit file changes.

**Plant.** `plants/mujoco/single.build_single_model_backend(cfg, control_period=...)` is the single-model MuJoCo plant a robot
config describes (`sim_model_path`, `sim_timestep`, `sim_default_joint_positions`, `sim_gravcomp`, the arm block), extracted
verbatim from the Dora `mujoco_interface` node, which now calls it too (one builder, not two).

**Servo semantics SimLink models** (the part of the RT server the supervisor depends on): ARM starts a new, never-reused ARM
epoch and holds the measured pose (`FLAG_HOLDING`) until commands flow; DISARM bumps the stop epoch and clears a fault; a
command is accepted only while armed and not faulted and `last_cmd_seq` is the last accepted one (the APPLIED rule reads it);
the deadman holds the measured pose after `hold_ms` and latches `FAULT_CMD_LOST` after `fault_ms`, ARM is refused while
latched; a plant exception latches `FAULT_PLANT`. All servo parameters come from the config's `sim_servo` block
(`state_hz`, `hold_ms`, `fault_ms`, `hold_kp`, `hold_kd`) with no defaults: a simulation that is not told them does not invent
them (the bench uses the fake-RT bench's own launch values).

**DISARM releases the plant.** A disarmed servo writes an explicit zero-gain, zero-torque word (`MuJoCoBackend.step(None)` would
keep re-applying its last command, so passing nothing is not releasing); the plant's own passive support (gravity compensation
where configured) stays. A DISARM or ARM is confirmed only by a state sample that was stepped under it: samples carry the
authority their physics ran in. A duplicate ARM is acknowledged without a new epoch; every DISARM bumps the stop epoch.
Holds use the gains of the last accepted command of the epoch (the configured hold gains only before any command), as the RT
server does; the published `stop_epoch` advances only in a sample whose step ran released (a DISARM and ARM inside one step show
neither, and the DISARM is reported unconfirmed). `sim_servo` is validated like the RT server's launch flags: finite numbers, `state_hz` 1..1000, `0 < hold_ms <
fault_ms`, non-negative gains. No shipped config has a `sim_servo` block yet, so `--plant sim` refuses every existing config
until one is written.

**Differences from the RT server, stated:** `tau` and `tau_cmd` in a state sample are the PD word the plant was given (before
the compiled servo law's clamp and slew), not a measured or post-clamp torque; `online_mask` is always 0; a plant failure latches
`FAULT_PLANT`, is PUBLISHED (from the last good pose, so the stream does not go stale) and the servo then releases the plant with
a zero word, as the RT server writes nothing in that case (never reported as HOLDING); the identity reads
`rt:sim`; the trace's `rt_host` is the rt block's host. The sim steps and runs its deadman at `state_hz` (4 ms at 250 Hz), where the RT servo runs at 1 kHz and publishes at `state_hz`. Sim time follows the wall clock only while a step fits its period (a
viewer or a composed scene can overrun it), while the deadman and the executor stay on wall time. If the servo thread does not
stop within 2 s `close()` leaves the plant open rather than close it under a running step.

**Not modelled:** native reference (v7), so `stream` and `group` are not advertised and refuse; pose-hold and Cartesian
commands are refused; no clock domain (`state_history` reports `clock_domain: None`, so paired feedback across two simulated
arms is refused); no RT-side torque clamp or slew beyond what the plant itself applies (the MuJoCo plant runs the compiled servo
law when `arm_rt_servo` is present). It is a different plant from the RT server and says so; passing here is not a claim that
sim and hardware behave alike.

**Open decision, found while scoping the graphs (not built):** the Dora sim graphs (`sim_bench`, `sim_workcell_add`) do not use
a single-model plant. They run a composed scene (arm, base, objects, welds, Hand and a grasp gate) hosted by the project's
`mujoco_interface` node, and perception, the planner and the viewer read the scene's full state from it (`sim_qpos`,
`inhand_pose`). One MuJoCo world has one owner, so moving such a graph under a supervisor needs a decision: the supervisor
hosts the scene and the scene state reaches those readers through an observer channel; or the scene stays in a project process
and the supervisor's link is a client of it. SimLink does not decide that. A dual simulated pair has the same question (two
supervisors cannot share one world).

Bench: `tools/bench/check_sim_supervisor.py` (offline suite): the servo semantics in process, the builder's keys, and the
supervisor process driven through the SDK (extensions, claim/arm, a move through SENT and APPLIED, cancel, DISARM and a new ARM
epoch, `state_history` and `trace` reads, no stream or group).
