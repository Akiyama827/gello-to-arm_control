# The FR3 profile: one home for the robot's facts (config single source, step 1)

Operator instruction 2026-10-06: one source of truth for configs. Plan: `Control/docs/status/2026-10-06-config-single-source-plan.md`.

`robots/fr3/` states the Franka Research 3 once: `description.yaml` (joints, finger joints, TCP frame, URDF, factory torque ratings,
motor and render names), `gains.yaml` (default stiffness/damping, with the kd sweep that justifies it), `execution.yaml` (the admission
policy: THE statement of the FR3's speed and acceleration limits) and `sim.yaml` (the single-model MuJoCo plant). The example configs
include them and state only example choices; the project's configs include them in step 2.

Behaviour that follows from one statement:
* **Speed limits.** The console's planner times plans at the execution policy's limits when it states none (`planner_speed_limits`), and a
  planner limit above the policy is refused at load (it used to plan at 0.5 rad/s / 1.0 rad/s^2 and the supervisor's admission at
  0.4 / 0.8 refused the plans). The example robot now carries the FR3 policy, so the stock in-process console enforces it too.
* **Mode profiles include.** `ARM_CONTROL_MODE_CONFIG`, and the supervised console's merged profiles, are loaded with the
  config-tree loader, so a mode can include a shared one.
* **One speed-limit rule for every reader.** `node_utils.planner_speed_limits` (moved from the console) is used by the console's
  planner and by the replay node's acceleration cap: the policy when stated, a planner statement only if not faster, else the
  historical 0.5 / 1.0. Replay used to require a policy or `planner.acc_limits`; with the example mode's planner limits gone, a
  project sim entry with no policy would have refused to start (review round 1). `arm.max_vel/max_acc` (the planning stack and the
  dual-arm console) follow in the next slice.
* **The FR3's rotor inertia in the single-model plant.** `robots/fr3/sim.yaml` states `sim_armature: 0.1` (Franka's published MJCF
  value, the one the workcell plant applies as `ARM_ARMATURE`) and the single-model plant applies a robot's stated armature to its
  arm joints (fingers excluded); a robot that states none keeps its description's own (review round 1: an unscoped default changed
  the Assembler twin's dynamics). That was the root cause of the FR3 wrist
  instability behind the 0.5 ms decision: without it a move left a 0.05 rad/s limit cycle on joint 7 even at 0.5 ms and the console's
  settling check failed in both the stock and the supervised graph; with it the arm settles to ~1e-6 rad/s, and both graphs complete
  their moves (verified live, headless). The 0.5 ms step stays (operator decision).
* **Slew per RT tick.** The servo law's torque slew (1 N*m) is applied per plant step; it is now scaled by step/1 ms, so the twin slews at
  the RT server's 1000 N*m/s at any step (at 0.5 ms it had slewed twice as fast).

Checked: `tools/inspect/config_snapshot.py` (parent repo) shows only the intended resolved-config changes (the example robot gains the FR3
execution policy; the example planner's 0.5/1.0 is gone; `sim_supervised.yaml` no longer restates a policy); the moved description,
gains and sim keys resolve identically. Benches: `check_sim_supervisor.py` (armature applied, slew per step), `arm_console --self-check`
(planner limits follow the policy).

**Open defect found while verifying (pre-existing; not fixed here, operator decision pending).** A goal exactly on a URDF joint limit
faults the controller with "non-finite or invalid servo command". The cubic Hermite evaluator (`JointTrajectory.sample_at`, and the
byte-parity C++ twin `rt/include/arm_rt/reference.hpp`) evaluates a flat segment held at q as `(h00+h01)*q`, which is one ulp past q
for some s (offline: a segment held at -0.1518 sampled 35 of 2001 times at -0.15179999999999996), and `ExecutionPolicy.command_valid`
compares the position envelope strictly. Measured live (supervised graph, joint 4 driven to its upper limit -0.1518): the second move
faulted in 6 of 9 runs; the fault line carried q_des[3] = -0.15179999999999996 against an upper bound of -0.1518. This is the
"stopped: non-finite or invalid servo command" seen once in the bridge review. This slice widens exposure: the stock example console
now runs the FR3 execution policy, so its in-process controller applies the same check. Owner: control backend; blocking effect: any
plan whose goal sits on a joint limit can fault at execution (fail-safe: the arm holds), on any backend that applies the envelope.

Review round 2 (ACCEPT) noted two replay semantics, stated here: a mode profile's `planner.acc_limits` faster than the robot's policy now
refuses replay at startup (the policy used to win silently), and a robot stating neither a policy nor a planner limit gets 1.0 rad/s^2
where replay used to refuse (no config in either repo is in that case today). The joint-limit defect is fixed in its own slice
(2026-10-06-limit-rounding.md).
